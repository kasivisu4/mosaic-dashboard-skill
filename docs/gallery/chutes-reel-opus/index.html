    About the data

    Source

    A production trace of the Chutes LLM inference platform: 6,122,413,756 requests over 367 days. Day 0 of the trace is 11 April 2025 (per the source paper, arXiv:2608.13573); data and code are at github.com/HarvardMadSys/chutes_workload.

    Aggregation

    • The public file rolls the requests up to one row per trace day × trace hour × model × endpoint function × streaming flag (1,337,094 rows), with request counts and sums with counts for input tokens, output tokens and time to first token.
    • This page embeds a smaller rollup, one row per day × hour × model family × request category (247,258 rows, about 4.8 MB), and computes everything in your browser with DuckDB.
    • Every average is a ratio of sums (for example total input tokens ÷ requests with an input-token count), so it stays correct under any filter.
    • Families: the five largest named families keep their own colour; the other nine (including models listed only by UUID and the long tail outside the top 200 models) are grouped as "Other families".

    How to read the scenes

    • One bead = 10 million requests, rounded per month (largest remainders, so a month's beads add up to its rounded total). A family-month under 5 million requests can show no bead; every bead's tooltip gives the exact figure.
    • "Requests a day" divides a month's requests by the days present in the trace, so the partial first month (20 days) and last month (12 days) compare fairly. Partial months are marked on every month scene.
    • Scene titles describe all the data. With a filter on, the marks and numbers follow it; the titles do not.
    • Month and day scenes keep the whole year in view when you pick dates (they dim what lies outside); the KPI numbers, the time strip and the hour scenes count only your dates.

    Limitations

    • Hour of day is the trace's own clock; the source states no time zone, so hour patterns are relative.
    • 13 January 2026 has only 3 of 24 hours and 14 January 15 hours: incomplete logging, not a drop in demand.
    • Token counts exist only for text endpoints; time to first token only for streaming calls (about 92% of streaming requests report it).
    • The trace counts requests; it says nothing about users, prices or answer quality. Model-level detail (201 models) and the streaming split are not in this page's rollup.
    • Not drawn: reasoning-type models rose from 17.1% of requests in May 2025 to 44.7% in November 2025 and fell to 7.2% in March 2026.
